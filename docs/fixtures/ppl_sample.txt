The sky is blue because of a phenomenon called Rayleigh scattering. When sunlight enters the atmosphere, it collides with gas molecules. Sunlight is made up of all colors of the rainbow, each with a different wavelength. Blue light has a shorter wavelength than red light, which means it scatters more easily when it strikes molecules in the atmosphere. This scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions across the sky, making it appear blue. At sunrise and sunset, sunlight has to travel through more of the atmosphere to reach our eyes, so most of the blue light has already been scattered away by the time it reaches us. What remains is the longer wavelength light, the reds and oranges, which gives the sky its characteristic colors at those times of day.

The speed of light in a vacuum is approximately 299,792 kilometers per second, often approximated as 300,000 kilometers per second. This constant, denoted by the letter c, is one of the most fundamental constants in physics. Nothing with mass can reach or exceed this speed. As an object with mass accelerates toward the speed of light, it requires ever-increasing amounts of energy. At the speed of light itself, the energy required would be infinite.

Water is a molecule consisting of two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom, giving it the chemical formula H2O. Pure water is colorless, odorless, and tasteless. It exists in three states of matter: solid ice, liquid water, and water vapor. Water is an excellent solvent, often called the universal solvent, because many substances dissolve in it. This property makes water essential for biological processes, as it transports nutrients and waste products in living organisms.

The human brain contains approximately 86 billion neurons, each connected to thousands of other neurons through synapses. These connections allow the brain to process information, form memories, and control the body. The brain is divided into several major regions, including the cerebral cortex, which handles higher cognitive functions such as thinking, perception, and language; the cerebellum, which coordinates movement; and the brainstem, which controls basic life functions like breathing and heart rate.
